Successful ecommerce projects bring together business requirements, customer experience, and technology delivery. They require clear ownership and coordinated input from teams across the organization.

  • Delivery approach: Agree on a predictive, Agile, or hybrid approach at the outset. Align project governance and delivery practices with the scope, complexity, and needs of the business.
  • Requirements gathering: Use stakeholder interviews, workshops, wireframes, and prototypes to identify business and customer needs. Document these requirements so teams share a clear understanding of the expected outcomes.
  • Product specifications: Maintain product documentation as the agreed reference for development. Keep specifications current as requirements and priorities evolve.
  • Feature definition: Translate business requirements into clearly defined product features and actionable development tasks.
  • User experience design: Use customer personas and user stories to guide the design of websites, mobile applications, and other customer interactions.
  • Systems architecture: Define the technical architecture, including build-or-buy decisions, platform selection, integrations, and vendor dependencies.
  • Source control: Manage application code and configuration in a version control system such as Git. Maintain a clear history of changes to support collaboration and release management.
  • Project management: Organize work into defined projects and tasks with clear responsibilities across engineering, operations, and marketing. Track progress against agreed deliverables.
  • Delivery planning: Develop realistic schedules based on the work breakdown, available resources, dependencies, and budget.
  • Stakeholder communication: Establish a communication plan for internal teams, partners, and vendors. Use status reports, demonstrations, and delivery plans to communicate progress, risks, and decisions.
  • Software development: Configure or develop the required capabilities within the chosen technology environment. Coordinate engineering teams around iterative delivery while maintaining alignment with product specifications and broader project milestones.
  • Documentation: Maintain technical and business documentation throughout delivery, from requirements and architecture to budgets and project plans. Prepare test plans, user guidance, and support materials before release.
  • Quality assurance: Integrate testing into the development workflow. Use business acceptance testing to confirm that releases meet agreed requirements before deployment.
  • Team management: Lead cross-functional teams with clear communication, technical understanding, and empathy. Establish accountability and help teams resolve dependencies.
  • Catalog and content management: Treat the product catalog as a dedicated workstream with clear ownership and disciplined quality control. Coordinate contributions from product teams, marketing, and vendors.
  • Organic and paid search: Align content and acquisition campaigns with the customer journey. Connect search traffic and referral links to relevant products and clear purchase opportunities.
  • Budget management: Monitor spending across people, software, infrastructure, and delivery time. Make explicit trade-offs between scope, cost, and quality as constraints change.
  • Hands-on leadership: Remove obstacles, resolve competing priorities, and provide practical support where the team needs it. Take responsibility for decisions that keep delivery moving.
  • Commercial performance: Use sales performance as the primary measure of commercial success. Monitor supporting indicators such as cart abandonment, order value, site engagement, and page performance to guide improvements.
